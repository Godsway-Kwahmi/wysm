Two-Day Training Workshop for Traditional Leaders and Community Leaders on Responding to Migrants Experiencing Gender-Based Violence (GBV)

Accra — 27–28 August 2026, Cedi Conference Centre, University of Ghana

Ho — 30–31 July 2026, Hotel Stevens

When You See Me works closely with leaders who can be influential in their communities. Queenmothers from the Greater Accra and Volta Regions, as well as Nigerian community leaders, participated in the training. In addition to discussing the relevant laws, role play exercises were used to model how traditional and community leaders should treat survivors of violence. The workshops were led by Dr. Angela Dwamena-Aboagye and Dr. Adolf Bekoe of the Ark Foundation.

Policy Workshop

13 July 2026, ISSER Conference Centre, University of Ghana

When You See Me seeks to impact policy and practice. Engagement with civil society and policy bodies is therefore an important part of its activities. This policy workshop brought together representatives from several agencies, including the Ghana Immigration Service, the Domestic Violence and Victim Support Unit of the Ghana Police Service, the Centre for Gender Studies and Advocacy, the Ark Foundation, the Migration Advocacy Centre, the DA Foundation, and Drama Queens, to discuss how the findings of the research project, ‘Queenmothers, Migrants, and Gender-Based Violence in Ghana,’ can inform policy and practice. The outputs from this workshop include a flyer for migrants that lists the contact information of organisations that support survivors of gender-based violence.

Launch of Short Story

16 July 2026, Kata Gardens Bistro, Accra

When You See Me works with artists to raise awareness of gender-based violence. The launch of the short story featured a reading by Peace Adzo Medie and performances by the poet, Twita, and the vocalist, falè. The Lododo Art Foundation also performed excerpts from the play adaptation.
