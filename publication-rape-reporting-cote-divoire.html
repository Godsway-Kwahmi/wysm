Rape Reporting in Post-Conflict Côte d’Ivoire: Accessing Justice and Ending Impunity

Journal article · 2017

Drawing on interviews with survivors, police officers and gendarmes, this article asks why women who were raped during and after Côte d’Ivoire’s civil conflict did, or did not, report it to the authorities. It finds that support from family and friends made survivors more likely to come forward, while the blame and shame directed at them by their own communities discouraged it, and considers what this means for ending impunity in post-conflict states.

African Affairs, Vol. 116, No. 464 (2017), pp. 414–434. DOI: 10.1093/afraf/adx008
