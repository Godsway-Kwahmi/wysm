About

Preventing and responding to gender-based violence in migrant communities in Ghana.

Migration is a significant risk factor to experiencing gender-based violence (GBV) and women who migrate without their relatives often lack help to prevent the problem. This project is on the prevention of and responses to GBV within migrant communities in Ghana.

We will employ narrative methodology to study the help- and justice-seeking behaviours of female Nigerian immigrants in response to two forms of GBV – intimate partner violence (IPV) and non-partner perpetrated sexual violence. We will then use applied theatre and educational illustrated stories (comics) to raise awareness of how survivors can access services and justice and how their host community and female traditional leaders – ‘queenmothers’ – can assist them.

What the research looks at

The study follows female Nigerian immigrants in Ghana and the choices they make after violence: who they tell, where they go for help, and what happens when they try to reach services or the courts.

Narrative methods keep those accounts in the words of the women who lived them, rather than reducing them to a set of categories fixed in advance.

Three strands of work

Narrative research

Life-story interviews with women who have experienced intimate partner violence and non-partner perpetrated sexual violence after migrating.

Applied theatre

Performances made with and for migrant communities, opening a way to talk about violence and about the help that is available.

Illustrated stories

Educational comics that show how survivors can reach services and justice, and how neighbours and queenmothers can help them get there.
