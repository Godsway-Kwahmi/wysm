Before anything else

How the site is put together

The website has two halves, and only one of them is yours to work in.

Yours, in Sanity Studio

The words and the photographs. Every publication in the list, every caption, every paragraph of introduction, the email address in the footer, the names of the funders. You change these, you press Publish, and the website changes.

Not yours, with a developer

Where things sit on the page, how large they are, the colours, the type, the photographs behind the words on the opening screen, and any page that does not exist yet. These live in code, and a small change in one place moves things on every screen.

Getting started

Your first time in Studio

  1. Open the address on the front page of this guide. Studio loads in the browser like any other site. Nothing needs installing.
  2. Sign in with the account on the project's workspace.
  3. Find the menu down the left. It lists the pages by name, plus two things that are not pages: Site settings and Creative work.
  4. Click a name. A list opens. Click one entry to open it, or the button at the top of the list to start a new one.

The two buttons, and the difference

Save draft keeps it for yourself. The website does not change and nobody else sees it. Use it whenever you are mid-sentence.

Publish puts it on the website. Within a minute or two, anyone opening the page sees your words.

There is no undo on Publish. Publish the fix and it is gone. That is normal, not a disaster.

Four habits that prevent every accident

Never delete an entry you did not create. Deleting a publication takes it off the site at once.

Change one thing, then publish. If six changed at once, you will not know which one broke.

Look at the site after you publish, with Shift held on the reload key, so it is not a cached copy.

If a field is greyed out or a button is missing, your account cannot see it. Ask whoever set you up; you are not doing it wrong.

Add a publication

The one catch

A new publication joins the list. It does not get a page.

Each publication's own page — the one that opens when a reader clicks it, with the cover, the abstract and the download button — is built separately from the list. Adding a sixth publication in Studio puts a sixth row on the Publications page, and that row will open a page that does not exist yet.

So: before you add a publication, ask for its page. Send the title, the abstract, the year and the cover, and say it is a new publication. The page is quick to make. Then the Link field in Studio gets filled in to point at it.

To add one, once the page exists

  1. Open Publications in the left-hand menu.
  2. Click the button at the top of the list that starts a new entry.
  3. Fill the fields from the previous page of this guide, top to bottom.
  4. Paste the page address into Link when the developer gives it you.
  5. Press Publish.
  6. Open the Publications page on the website and check the new row is there, in the right year, with the right cover.

To change which publication is shown large at the top of the page

Open Publications page (not an individual publication) and use the Featured picker. Leave it empty and the website shows the newest one by itself, which is the right answer nearly always.

To change the order

The list sorts by date, newest first. Two publications in the same year appear in the order the project decided, which is held by the developer — tell them if you need that changed.

The everyday edit

Change words on a page

  1. Open the page's name in the left-hand menu.
  2. Click into the field and make your change. Double-click a word to select it; three clicks take the whole sentence.
  3. Press Publish.
  4. Look at the website.

Paragraphs

Studio's text fields are plain. To start a new paragraph, leave a whole empty line between the two. One line break is not a new paragraph — it just wraps.

Copying in from Word or a PDF

Paste, then look at what arrived. Invisible formatting travels with the text: curly quotation marks that print as odd characters, stray spaces, a bold you did not ask for. If the field has a button that says clear formatting, use it. Better still, paste into the field, then retype the two or three places it looks wrong.

What each field actually is

Welcome statementThe sentence on the opening page, beside the line above it
Standfirst / SummaryThe one line directly under a page's title
IntroductionThe paragraph under that on Creative Works
List headingThe words over the list of publications
Gallery headingThe words over the photographs
Form promptThe words beside the box on Contact

A field left empty does not break the page: the website falls back to the words written into it. That is why something you changed may look right in Studio and still show the old sentence — check you published the right field, then ask.

Photographs

Change or add a photograph

  1. Click the image field. If one is already there, take it out first.
  2. Drag the file in, or choose it from your computer.
  3. When the upload finishes, a crop box appears. Drag it to frame the part that matters — the people, not the empty wall. Only move it if the default framing is wrong.
  4. Write the alt text. This is not optional, and it is the part most people skip.
  5. Publish, then look at the page.

Alt text, in one rule

Say what is in the picture to someone who cannot see it, in one sentence, and do not write "photo of" or "image of".

NoCommunity theatre image.jpg
NoWomen performing
YesFive performers in ankara cloth seated in a row on a stage before a red drape, one standing and gesturing, the audience's heads along the bottom of the frame.

If the photograph carries words that matter — a book cover, a banner — include them.

Files

A photograph from a phone is fine. A very large file makes the page slow, and a very small one looks blurred. If you are asked to send the original, send the original.

Two things not to do

Do not add text into the photograph itself — captions belong in the words beside it, where they can be read by everyone and resized.

Do not put a photograph of a person's face where the website expects a document cover, or the other way round. The frame they go into is sized for one or the other.

Being found

The search fields

Every page has a small section for search. Two lines do most of the work of how the project looks to someone who has never heard of it.

FieldWhere it showsWhat to write
TitleThe blue line in Google's results, and the name of the browser tabUnder about 60 characters. The page's subject first, the project's name last. Leave it empty to use what the page already says.
DescriptionThe two lines under itOne or two sentences, under about 155 characters, that say what a reader will find. Write it for a person deciding whether to click.

Three rules

Do not leave a field half-filled. A title with the description still blank makes the page read worse than both being blank.

Do not stuff it with words. "Publications, gender-based violence, Ghana, research, theatre, short story, Medie" reads as noise to a person and to a search engine.

Never repeat the same description on two pages. Each page should say something different.

The limit, so you are not surprised

These fields reach Google and the browser tab. They do not reach the card that appears when a page is shared on WhatsApp, Facebook or LinkedIn. That card is written by the developer, page by page, and it shows the publication's own cover.

So if you change a description and the shared card still says the old thing, you have not done it wrong. Ask for the card to be updated to match.

One field to fix right now

The Creative Works page's search description currently reads "Applied theatre and illustrated stories made with migrant communities in Ghana…". The page itself, and the project's own brief, describe theatre, a short story and illustrated comics. The sentence should match. That is a two-minute change in the Creative Works page's search fields.

If you are not sure which list something is on

Write it, publish it, and look at the website. If nothing changed, it is on one of these two lists. Nothing is lost by trying: Studio keeps what you wrote, and it will arrive the day the page is connected to it.

Something looks wrong on the site

  1. Reload with Shift held down. The most common cause is a saved old copy of the page.
  2. Did you press Publish, not Save draft? The button at the top tells you which state you are in.
  3. Is the field empty? An empty field shows the page's own words, not a blank space.
  4. Is it on the page 11 or 12 list? If so, it cannot arrive yet.
  5. Wait two minutes. The site rebuilds itself after a publish.
  6. Still wrong? Send three things: the page's address, a screenshot of what you wrote in Studio, and a screenshot of what the website shows. That is enough to find almost anything.

When in doubt

Stop and ask. Nothing in Studio is lost by waiting, and a question costs less than undoing something that has been published.
